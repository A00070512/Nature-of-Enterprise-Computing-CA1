Computing Evolution: A Multi-Perspective Analysis

## 2 historical development:

modern technology has progressed through a number of significant developments in computer hardware. From basic mechanical computers, to today's personal computers, the following are some of the important stages in this process.

### 2.1 mechanical and early programmable foundations:

the history of the modern computer goes back to the 19th century from british mathematician charles babbage. in 1822 babbage conceived of the difference engine,
a mathematical calculator designed to perform automatic polynomial computations.
based on the same idea, babbage introduced in 1837 the idea of an analytical engine, which is considered to be the prototype of the first computer.
although not built during his time, ada lovelace understood its significance and created the first algorithm for the machine.
almost a hundred years later german engineer konrad zuse created the Z3 in 1941, which was the first working electromechanical, fully automatic programmable digital computer

### 2.2 first generation vacuum tube systems:

the second world war spedup development in computer technology, which led to the first generation of electronic digital computers using vacuum tubes.
In 1943, Colossus, an electronic computer designed for code breaking was invented by british engineers. in 1945, ENIAC (electronic numerical integrator and computer), designed by John Mauchly and J. Presper Eckert from the usa, was ready.
ENIAC was capable of computing thousands of operations per second and filled a whole room, its task changes involved manual rewiring. In 1951 the first commercial electronic digital computer, the UNIVAC I, was introduced to the american market, proving that computing can be used for civilian purposes and not only for military studies

### 2.3 second generation translator and third generation integrated circuit systems:

During the 1950s, transistors were developed as an alternative to large, unreliable and power hungry vacuum tubes. Developed at Bell Labs, these small solid‑state components enabled efficient switching and completed the same functions as vacuum tubes but in a much smaller and more economical format (Computer History Museum, n.d.). Computers, such as the IBM 1401, therefore became smaller, quicker, more dependable and more economical to service, meaning that more companies, banks and universities began to implement mainframe computers (IBM, n.d.).

through the 1960s, the creation of the circuit (IC) signalled the third generation of computers. engineers were now able to embed transistors, resistors and capacitors onto one silicon chip, which vastly improved computational speed and further decreased production expenses (Encyclopaedia Britannica, n.d.). Moreover, computer systems such as the IBM System/360 pioneered a range of computer sizes which utilised computer software. this solidified business computer usage within institutions and also established the basis for todays computer software environment (IBM, n.d.).

### 2.4 4th generation microprossesors and the rise of personal computing

the fourth generation of computing began in 1971 when Intel released the 4004, the worlds first commercially available singlechip microprocessor (Intel, n.d.). by shrinking an entire central processing unit (CPU) onto one tiny silicon chip, microprocessors drastically reduced hardware costs, power requirements, and physical size.

this breakthrough fundamentally changed who could own and operate a computer. instead of requiring multimillion-dollar budgets and dedicated air-conditioned rooms, computing power became affordable enough for hobbyists, engineers, and startups to build compact personal computers (Computer History Museum, n.d.). early microcomputers like the Altair 8800 and later consumer systems from Apple and IBM brought computing directly into homes, schools, and small businesses, completing the historical transition from massive institutional mainframes to everyday personal computers (Encyclopaedia Britannica, n.d.; Science Museum, 2023).
