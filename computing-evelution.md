## 2  historical development:

modern technology has progressed through a number of significant developments in computer hardware. From basic mechanical computers, to todays personal computers, the following are some of the important stages in this process.

### 2.1  mechanical and early programmable foundations:

the history of the modern computer goes back to the 19th century from brithish mathematician charles babbage. in 1822 babbage conceived of the difference engine,
a mathematical calculator designed to perform automatic polynommial computattions.
based on the same idea, babbage introduced in 1837 the idea of an analytical engine, which is considered to be the prototype of the first computer.
although not built during his time, ada lovelace understood its significance and created the first algorithm for the machine.
almost a hundred years later german engineer konrad zuse created the Z3 in 1941, which was the first working electromechanical, fully automatic programmable digital computer

### 2.2  1st generation vacuum tube systems:

the second world war spedup development in computer technology, which led to the first generation of electronic digital computers using vacuum tubes.
In 1943, Colossus, an electronic computer designed for code breaking was invented by british enginears. in 1945, ENIAC (electronic numerical integrator and computer), designed by John
Mauchly and J Presper Eckert from the usa, was ready.
ENIAC was capable of computing thousands of operations per second and filled a whole room, its task changes involved manual rewiring. In 1951 the first commercial electronic digital
computer, the UNIVAC I, was introduced to the american market, proving that computing can be used for civilian purposes and not only for military studies

### 2.3  2nd generation translator and third generation integrated circuit systems:

during the 1950s, transistors were developed as an alternative to large unreliable and power hungry vacuum tubes. developed at bell labs, these small solid state components enabled
efficient switching and completed the same functions as vacuum tubes but in a much smaller and more economical format (computer history museum). computers such as the IBM 1401,
therefore became smaller, quicker, more dependable and more economical to service, meaning that more companies, banks and universities began to implement mainframe computers (IBM)

through the 1960s, the creation of the circuit (IC) signaled the third generation of computers. enginears were now able to embed transistors, resistors and capacitors onto one silicon
chip, which improved proccesing speed and further decreased production expenses (encyclopaedia britannica). however computer systems such as the IBM System/360 introduced a range of
computer sizes which ran on computer software. this secured business computer usage within institutions and also established the basis for todays computer software environment (IBM)

### 2.4  4th generation microprossesors and the rise of personal computing

the 4th generation of computing begun in 1971 when intel released the 4004 witch is the worlds first commercially available singlechip microprocessor (intel) by shrinking an entire
central processing unit (wich is the cpu) onto one tiny silicon chip, microprocessors reduced hardware costs, power requirements and size.

this breakthrough changed who could own and use a computer. instead of requiring multimillion dollar budgets and air conditioned rooms, computing power became affordable enough for daly
use, engineers, and startups to build compact personal computers (computer history museum) early microcomputers like the altair 8800 and later consumer systems from apple and IBM
brought computing directly into homes, schools, and small businesses, completing the historical transition from massive institutional mainframes to everyday personal computers
(encyclopaedia britannica, science museum 2023)
