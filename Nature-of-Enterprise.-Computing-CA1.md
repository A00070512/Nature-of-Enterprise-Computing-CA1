# Nature-of-Enterprise-Computing-CA1
Computing Evolution: From mainframes to personal computers (IBM to Apple/Microsoft)

Introduction:

The evolution of computer from large mainframe systems to personal computer is one of the most significant technological transformations in modern history. In today’s world, computers are an important part of our everyday life, helping people to communicate, work, study, shop and keep up with everything that’s happening all across the globe, instantly. However, this level of accessibility was not always like this. In earlier years of computing, computers were massive machines that occupied entire rooms and were only able to be access by government organisations. These systems, led by companies like IBM, were powerful but expensive and only available to a limited amount of users. As technology advanced, the focus shifted from big institutional computers to smaller, easier to afford personal computers that could use nearly everywhere from your comfort of your home, in schools and workplaces.

This transformation was significant because it changed the way people interacted with technology. Before personal computers became widely available, most people had little to no direct experience using computers. Mainframe systems were generally operated by trained professional who understood how to manage and process large amounts of information. They were mainly designed to handle complicated calculations, store important records and process information for organisations. Although these computers played an important role in the development of modern technology, they weren't designed for everyday personal use. As a result, access to computing was limited, and most individuals depended on business, school, other organisations, etc to access computer-based services.

The development of personal computers marked a major turning point in technological history, as it allowed computing to become part of everyday life rather than something restricted to large organisations. This change was important not only because computers became smaller and more affordable, but also because it gave people greater independence and opportunities to use technology for their own needs. It transformed education, business, communication and entertainment, making many everyday activities quicker and more convenient also helped create the foundations for the digital society we live in today, where having access to a computer or similar devices is often considered essential.

The main viewpoint explored in this case study is the development of personal computers was much more than a technological improvement. It was represented a major shift in how computing power was distributed throughout society. While mainframe computers provided organisation with the ability to process large amounts of information, personal computers allowed people to access similar technology for their own personal, educational and professional needs. This case study will examine challenges of transition, including its influence on education businesses, communication and everyday life. By looking at these developments, it aims to demonstrate how the move of mainframe systems helped create the foundation of the modern digital world of computing.
